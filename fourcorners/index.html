Four-Corner Fact Check

We divide students into teams (likely 4-5); assigning pinnies would make scoring easier. Teams must move as a unit.

Four corners: True, False, Unproven, No Idea. I read a claim; teams have twenty seconds to select a corner. Then we go to VAR.

Teacher notes
  • Before class, click the print button above and tape one sign in each corner of the gym.
  • Roles: VAR (librarian) reads each claim, starts the shot clock, and runs the review. The Scorekeeper’s Table (PE teacher) splits the class into four or five teams and awards points: 2 for the right corner, 1 for No Idea.
  • Pace: nine claims at about five minutes each. Claim 9, about basketball, is the closer; if time runs short, skip from claim 7 to claim 9.
  • Every review ends with a question for the room. One quick answer from a student is sufficient to move on. We’re looking to drill a pair of questions about wellness claims: who tested it and who was tested?
  • Claim 8 has two right corners. The research found no useful effect, but it studied only young army recruits.
  • Arrow keys move between claims; V opens the review. Scores and team names save on the device you’re using.
  • Right corner: 2 points. No Idea: 1 point, because I don’t reward unwarranted confidence. Wrong corner: 0.
  • We don’t tell students how the scoring works.
