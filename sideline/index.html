The Presidential Fitness Test

Document 1 | 2025

The Order

On July 31, 2025, President Trump signed an executive order titled “President’s Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition, and the Reestablishment of the Presidential Fitness Test.” The test, it says, “shall be administered by the Secretary of Health and Human Services with the support of the Secretary of Education.”

This order reestablishes the test. What does that word tell you about the test’s history?

Document 2 | 2013

The Replacement

After the 2012-2013 school year, the old test was phased out. Its replacement, the Presidential Youth Fitness Program, “minimizes comparisons between children and instead supports students as they pursue personal fitness goals,” in the government’s own description.

What did the replacement stop doing?

Document 3 | 1966

The Award

In 1966, under President Lyndon Johnson, the government created the Presidential Physical Fitness Award. Students who scored at or above the 85th percentile on every event earned it.

At the 85th percentile, you score higher than 85 percent of students. At most, what share of students could earn the award?

Document 4 | Over the years

The Events

The events tested changed over time. The version most people remember included a one-mile run, pull-ups or push-ups, sit-ups, a shuttle run, and the sit-and-reach.

Classify the events: which measure strength, which measure speed or endurance, and which measures flexibility?

Document 5 | 1956

The Council

On July 16, 1956, President Dwight Eisenhower created the President’s Council on Youth Fitness by executive order. He acted after a doctor’s study raised alarms about American kids.

A president created a council about kids’ fitness. What do you think alarmed him? Make a guess, then read the next document.

Document 6 | 1953

The Test That Started It

In 1953, Dr. Hans Kraus and Bonnie Prudden published an article warning about American kids’ fitness. Their test, the Kraus-Weber test, had six simple exercises. It was designed at a posture clinic in a New York hospital to screen for back trouble: a test of minimum muscular fitness. Kraus’s team gave it to about 4,400 American students and about 3,000 European students, and the American results set off alarms.

The first test checked for “minimum” fitness. The 1966 award went to the top scorers. What changed?

Make the Call

The test began as a check for the minimum, became a contest for the top 15 percent, then switched to personal goals. If your school could pick one version for your class, which should it be, and why?

Teacher notes

  • 1: It existed before and was stopped. 2: Comparing students with each other. 3: 15 percent at most, and fewer in practice, since the award took the 85th percentile on every event. 4: Strength: pull-ups or push-ups, sit-ups. Speed or endurance: the mile, the shuttle run. Flexibility: the sit-and-reach.
  • 5 and 6: A study comparing American and European students. What changed: the passing line moved from a minimum anyone healthy should clear to a bar only the top scorers could reach.
