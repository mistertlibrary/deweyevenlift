Core Strength

Dewey Even Lift?

Rainy days, test days, blown out ACLs and broken arms: PE loses both periods and students within periods.

Send them to the library or bring me down to the gym, and we can turn that time into games, true stories, and an abiding skepticism directed toward wellness claims.

Warm-Up

Time: 5:00.

We start class with an audacious claim. Students call Fact or Fiction, then we explore the data behind the prompt. Every Warm-Up ends with a question a Full Workout answers.

1 perPeriod

Full Workout

Period: 1 per.

We co-teach. In the gym, I’m VAR and you run the Scorekeeper’s Table; in Health, students trace a number back to where it started. In either case, the class moves, debates, and fact-checks against the figurative footage.

Sideline

Library: Pass.

For students who can’t participate: a short library dossier, solo or in pairs, tied to what their class is doing that day. They leave with a Securly pass and come back with something interesting to share.

How It Works

EventDone

  • Bad weather? Fields are closed and the gym is crowded. I bring the claim cards and the corner signs; you keep score.
  • Sitting out? Students with a note or an injury come to the library with a pass and work through a Sideline dossier. Their answers come back to you.
  • Health marking period: Single-period Full Workouts run in your room or the library.
  • Testing days and assemblies: book me ahead and bring whoever’s left.
  • Everything is ready to go; there’s nothing to grade unless your gradebook looks hungry.

Why It Counts

New Jersey’s Health standards ask students to “evaluate the validity of health information, resources, services, in school, home and in the community” (2.1.12.CHSS.6). All of my materials engage this very skill.

Curriculum and instruction should “include the integration of information literacy, consistent with the New Jersey Student Learning Standards and delivered in partnership by school library media specialists and classroom educators…” (N.J.A.C. 6A:8-6.1(c)).

What’s my inspiration? For one, a New York high school where the PE department and librarians developed library work for students who couldn’t participate in the hope of making a missed class meaningful and holding students to the standards.
